\section{Layout-Conditioned Surrogate Modeling and Local Adaptation}
\label{sec:problem_surrogate}

CASIO uses two complementary predictions to allocate structural evaluations. Feasibility predictions identify candidates unlikely to satisfy the design checks, while reinforcement-mass predictions support material-cost ranking. The models are trained across layouts and adapted to the current design task using evaluation feedback. Local adaptation comprises feasibility-score calibration and reinforcement residual correction. Here, ``local'' refers to the current layout and fixed design conditions. This section defines the engineering problem, candidate representation, and prediction models. Section~\ref{sec:optimization_framework} describes how these models guide candidate selection.

\subsection{Design Problem and Evaluation Scope}
\label{subsec:expensive_problem}

The task is to optimize section dimensions and concrete grade for a prescribed shear wall layout. Let $L$ denote the fixed wall and beam arrangement, $c$ the fixed design conditions, and $x\in\mathcal{X}$ a candidate design. The conditions include the number of stories, story height, seismic intensity, site class, seismic group, and loading assumptions. They remain fixed within each optimization task. The design variables comprise wall thicknesses in three vertical zones, main and secondary beam dimensions, and concrete grade. Their admissible discrete values define $\mathcal{X}$, with wall thicknesses constrained to be nonincreasing with height. Layout topology is not optimized.

Candidate evaluation comprises parametric structural modeling, FEA, reinforcement design, and code-based checking~\cite{GB500112010,GB500102010}. The feasibility label $y_f(x)$ equals 1 only if the analysis converges and the implemented structural and member-design checks pass. These checks cover interstory drift, torsional response, shear-to-weight ratio, vertical stiffness regularity, torsional-to-translational period ratio, wall axial compression, and wall and beam shear-compression limits. Analysis failure is treated as infeasibility, and the full implemented checking criteria are detailed in \ref{app:implementation_details}.

For a fixed $(L,c)$, the optimization problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\; C(x)&=\pi_s m_s(x)+\pi_c(x)m_c(x),\\
\text{subject to}\qquad y_f(x)&=1,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:optimization_problem}
\end{equation}
where $m_s$ and $m_c$ are reinforcement and concrete masses, $\pi_s$ is the reinforcement unit price, and $\pi_c(x)$ is the mass-based concrete unit price for the selected grade. The dependence on the fixed layout and conditions is omitted for readability. Here $C(x)$ represents the structural material cost, with unit prices and evaluation settings detailed in Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}.

Reinforcement demand depends on member forces and design checks and therefore requires structural analysis. Concrete quantity can instead be estimated analytically from member lengths, section dimensions, and story counts, denoted by $\widetilde{m}_c(x)$. CASIO consequently predicts feasibility and reinforcement mass, and combines the latter with $\widetilde{m}_c(x)$ for candidate ranking. Final design selection uses evaluated quantities and verified feasibility.

\subsection{Layout-Conditioned Candidate Representation}
\label{subsec:layout_parameter_model}

Each candidate is represented by $z=(G,d,q)$, where $G$ is a room-adjacency graph, $d$ contains the design parameters and conditions, and $q$ contains global layout descriptors (Fig.~\ref{fig:representation_variables}). For a fixed design task, $G$ and $q$ remain unchanged; only the section and material entries of $d$ vary during optimization.

The graph construction follows our earlier room-level representation for shear wall layout generation~\cite{LengRoomGNN2026}. Rooms form the nodes and shared boundaries define directed adjacency edges. The present task differs in that the wall arrangement is already known. The boundary-wise wall descriptors that served as generation targets in the earlier work are therefore included as inputs here. Each node has 41 features comprising nine normalized geometric descriptors, 16 boundary-availability indicators, and 16 descriptors of the prescribed wall arrangement. Each edge has eight attributes describing shared-boundary geometry, relative direction, and orientation. This representation associates the existing walls with the architectural spaces they bound, rather than treating room adjacency alone as a structural description.

The design vector contains eight numerical inputs (story count and seven section dimensions) and four categorical inputs (concrete grade, seismic intensity, site class, and seismic group). Numerical features are standardized using training-set statistics, and categorical variables are mapped to learned embeddings. Global context is supplied by 41 geometric and structural layout statistics, including building dimensions, directional wall lengths, wall eccentricity, beam quantities, and connectivity measures, together with seven room-graph summaries. The latter describe graph size, density, normalized room area, and aggregate wall distribution. These complementary descriptors connect local room organization with the overall structural configuration.

\subsection{Global Surrogate Models}
\label{subsec:surrogate_tasks}

FuseGNN combines graph and tabular information to predict candidate performance across layouts. A feasibility classifier $\Phi_f$ and a reinforcement regressor $\Phi_s$ are trained independently using the same architecture. Each network has its own graph encoder, tabular encoder, and output head, with no parameters shared between tasks. Their predictions are denoted by
\begin{equation}
p_g(z)=\Phi_f(z),\qquad m_g(z)=\Phi_s(z),
\label{eq:global_predictions}
\end{equation}
where $p_g$ is the global feasibility score in $[0,1]$ and $m_g$ is the predicted reinforcement mass.

The graph encoder applies three GraphSAGE layers~\cite{Hamilton2017GraphSAGE}, with residual connections and batch normalization. Mean and maximum pooling aggregate the node embeddings. A separate multilayer perceptron (MLP) encodes the edge attributes, which are mean-pooled to supplement the node representation. The tabular encoder jointly processes the design variables and the 41 layout statistics. Its output is concatenated with the pooled node and edge embeddings and the seven room-graph summaries, and an MLP head produces a scalar prediction. A sigmoid maps the classifier output to $p_g$, while the regressor output is transformed back to reinforcement mass. Fig.~\ref{fig:layout_parameter_surrogate} summarizes the prediction tasks. Network widths and training settings are specified in Section~\ref{subsec:global_surrogate_training}.

The classifier uses binary cross-entropy with the positive-class weight set to the ratio of negative to positive training samples. Since class weighting does not ensure calibrated probabilities, $p_g$ is initially used as a screening score. Its global screening threshold is selected on validation layouts to maximize the rejection rate while meeting a prescribed empirical recall for feasible candidates.

The reinforcement model uses Smooth L1 loss with transition parameter $0.5$ on the standardized log-mass target
\begin{equation}
u_s=\frac{\ln(1+m_s)-\mu_s}{\sigma_s},
\label{eq:steel_transform}
\end{equation}
where mass is expressed in kilograms and $\mu_s$ and $\sigma_s$ are calculated from the training targets. For a network output $\hat u_s$, the inverse transformation gives $m_g=\exp(\sigma_s\hat u_s+\mu_s)-1$. Samples with valid reinforcement quantities are used for regression, including designs that fail a structural check but yield a valid member-design result. Optimization uses the resulting mass estimates to rank candidates retained after screening.

\subsection{Online Local Adaptation}
\label{subsec:online_local_calibration}

A global surrogate trained across diverse layouts can exhibit systematic bias when applied to an unseen building, particularly as the optimization search concentrates within a local region of the design space. To mitigate this bias without retraining the global networks, CASIO updates lightweight local calibration models using FEA-verified samples accumulated during the current optimization run. At iteration $t$, let $\mathcal{D}_t$ denote the archive of unique designs evaluated so far along with their global predictions and true structural responses. Whenever new evaluations are added to $\mathcal{D}_t$, the local calibrators are refitted prior to assessing the next candidate batch.

\subsubsection{Feasibility calibration}
\label{subsubsec:feasibility_calibration}

To align global feasibility scores with the actual pass rate of the current layout, CASIO applies Platt scaling~\cite{Platt1999} using the logistic mapping
\begin{equation}
p_t(z)=\sigma\!\left(a_t\operatorname{logit}(p_g(z))+b_t\right),
\label{eq:calibrated_probability}
\end{equation}
where $a_t$ and $b_t$ are fitted to the local feasibility labels in $\mathcal{D}_t$ using $L_2$-regularized logistic regression, and the subscript $t$ denotes the calibrated prediction at iteration $t$. Because it estimates only two scalar parameters, Platt scaling is less prone to overfitting under limited online samples while effectively recalibrating the probability scale and screening threshold. Calibration is activated once $\mathcal{D}_t$ contains at least 25 evaluated designs spanning both feasible and infeasible classes. Before activation, the global score is retained ($p_t = p_g$).

\subsubsection{Reinforcement residual correction}
\label{subsubsec:steel_calibration}

For reinforcement mass prediction, a local regression model $\delta_t$ is trained to predict the residual $m_s - m_g$ using records in $\mathcal{D}_t$ that yield valid member-design results. The calibrated mass prediction is given by
\begin{equation}
m_t(z)=\max\{0,\;m_g(z)+\delta_t(d_{\mathrm{var}},m_g(z),\ln(1+m_g(z)))\},
\label{eq:calibrated_steel}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\mathrm{var}}$ denotes the active section and material variables for the current task. By learning only the layout-specific residual, $\delta_t$ preserves the cross-layout structural knowledge encoded in $m_g$ while correcting local systematic errors.

To balance model expressiveness against sample efficiency as $\mathcal{D}_t$ grows, $\delta_t$ follows a sample-count schedule in which ridge regression is used for small sample sets, Gaussian process regression for intermediate sets, and LightGBM~\cite{Ke2017LightGBM} for larger sets. Prior to reaching the minimum activation sample count, the global prediction is used directly ($m_t = m_g$).
