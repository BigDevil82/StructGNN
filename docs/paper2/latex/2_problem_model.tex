\section{Calibrated Surrogate Modeling Method for Shear Wall Structural Optimization}
\label{sec:problem_surrogate}

To improve the efficiency of shear wall section and material optimization, the proposed method uses surrogate models to assess candidate designs before FEA. It predicts candidate feasibility and reinforcement usage from a joint representation of layout and design parameters, and then calibrates these predictions online with FEA-verified samples from the current optimization task. The calibrated predictions are used to filter clearly low-feasibility candidates and rank promising low-cost candidates for true evaluation.

\subsection{Shear Wall Structural Optimization Problem Formulation}
\label{subsec:expensive_problem}

For a given shear wall layout, structural optimization aims to determine suitable section and material parameters while satisfying multiple design constraints. These parameters include wall thicknesses in different vertical zones, beam section dimensions, concrete strength grades, and other design-related variables. Although the layout of the structure is fixed, different parameter combinations may lead to substantially different structural responses, reinforcement demands, and material costs.

Let $L$ denote a fixed shear wall layout and $c$ denote the corresponding design condition, including seismic intensity, site class, seismic group, load condition, and other external design requirements. The optimization variables are denoted by $x \in \mathcal{X}$, where $\mathcal{X}$ is the discrete or mixed discrete design space formed by section and material parameters.

For each candidate design $(L,c,x)$, a true structural evaluator $\mathcal{E}$ is required to perform parametric modeling, FEA, structural response extraction, member design, and code-based checking. The evaluation output is written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(L,c,x) \rightarrow (y_f,m_s,m_c,r),
\label{eq:true_evaluator}
\end{equation}
where $y_f \in {0,1}$ is the comprehensive feasibility label, $m_s$ and $m_c$ denote the reinforcement and concrete quantities, respectively, and $r$ represents structural response and checking indicators such as inter-story drift ratio, torsional response, axial compression ratio, shear-compression ratio, and member design results. A candidate is regarded as feasible only when the analysis is successfully completed and all major global and member-level checks satisfy the code requirements.

The material cost of a feasible candidate is defined as
\begin{equation}
C(L,c,x)=p_s m_s(L,c,x)+p_c m_c(L,c,x),
\label{eq:material_cost}
\end{equation}
where $p_s$ and $p_c$ are the unit prices of reinforcement and concrete, respectively. In the experiments, these unit prices are determined according to current market prices. The optimization problem can therefore be formulated as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \quad & C(L,c,x), \
\mathrm{s.t.} \quad & y_f(L,c,x)=1 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:optimization_problem}
\end{equation}

The main computational difficulty lies in the repeated calls to $\mathcal{E}$. In particular, reinforcement usage is difficult to estimate accurately before FEA because it depends on internal force envelopes, member-level design, and code-based detailing requirements. Concrete quantity can be approximately obtained from geometric information and section dimensions. Therefore, this study focuses on two surrogate outputs that are most useful before true evaluation: the probability of passing comprehensive design checks and the reinforcement usage used for cost-related ranking.

\subsection{Surrogate Learning Tasks for FEA Budget Allocation}
\label{subsec:surrogate_tasks}

The surrogate models in this study support FEA budget allocation by providing two types of information before calling the true evaluator: whether a candidate is likely to be feasible, and whether it is likely to have low material cost.

\subsubsection{Feasibility screening}
\label{subsubsec:feasibility_task}

The feasibility surrogate estimates the probability that a candidate design can pass the comprehensive structural checks. Given the input representation $z$ of a candidate $(L,c,x)$, the feasibility surrogate $SM_f$ outputs
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{f}^{G}=SM_f(z),
\label{eq:feasibility_surrogate}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{p}_{f}^{G}\in[0,1]$ is the global surrogate prediction of feasibility probability.

This model is used as a conservative screening tool, with the main objective of rejecting clearly infeasible candidates while retaining most potentially feasible ones. Therefore, the threshold used in optimization is determined under a high feasible-recall requirement to control the risk of falsely rejecting feasible candidates.

\subsubsection{Reinforcement usage estimation}
\label{subsubsec:steel_task}

The reinforcement surrogate estimates the reinforcement quantity of a candidate design:
\begin{equation}
\hat{m}_{s}^{G}=SM_s(z),
\label{eq:steel_surrogate}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{m}_{s}^{G}$ is the global surrogate prediction of reinforcement usage.

This prediction provides a relative ranking signal for prioritizing candidates during pre-evaluation. Since concrete quantity can be rapidly approximated from geometry and section dimensions, a surrogate-based cost estimate can be written as
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}^{G}=p_s\hat{m}_{s}^{G}+p_c\tilde{m}_c,
\label{eq:global_surrogate_cost}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{m}_c$ is the estimated concrete quantity. Candidates with lower surrogate cost are assigned higher priority for true FEA evaluation.

\subsection{Layout-Conditioned Surrogate Models}
\label{subsec:layout_parameter_model}

The feasibility and reinforcement usage of a shear wall structure depend on both section and material parameters and the spatial organization of the layout. For the same design variables, different shear wall arrangements may result in different stiffness distributions, load paths, and seismic responses. Therefore, a surrogate model using only design parameters is insufficient for cross-layout candidate assessment.

This study uses a candidate representation conditioned on the structural layout. It combines a room-level graph, global layout statistics, and design parameters. The structural layout is represented as a graph
\begin{equation}
G=(V,E),
\label{eq:layout_graph}
\end{equation}
where rooms or spatial units are treated as nodes, and adjacency relationships between rooms are treated as edges. 
For each room node, a 25-dimensional feature vector is constructed by concatenating geometric descriptors and local constraint indicators. 
The geometric descriptors include the normalized bounding-box coordinates, bounding-box size, area, and centroid of the room, while the constraint indicators describe local buildable or wall-placement availability around the room. 
For each adjacency edge, an 8-dimensional attribute vector is used to describe the spatial relationship between two neighboring rooms, including the normalized shared-boundary length, the centroid of the shared boundary, relative direction indicators, and a boundary-orientation flag. 
All geometric quantities are normalized within each layout to reduce scale dependence. 
This room-level graph representation provides the surrogate model with local spatial organization, adjacency relationships, and potential wall-placement constraints without relying on manually defined room-function labels.

In addition to the room graph, a vector of global layout statistics $q$ is introduced to encode layout-level information, including building scale, wall quantity, directional wall ratio, boundary wall ratio, and other geometric or topological descriptors.

The candidate-specific design vector is denoted by $d$, which encodes the design condition $c$, the section and material parameters. The complete input representation of a candidate is then written as
\begin{equation}
z=\left(G,q,d\right)
\label{eq:candidate_representation}
\end{equation}

Based on this representation, a layout-conditioned graph surrogate model is used as the common architecture for the global surrogate models. The feasibility surrogate and the reinforcement surrogate are trained as two separate models with independent parameters. In each model, the graph encoder extracts a layout embedding from $G$, while the design encoder maps $d$ into a design-parameter embedding. These embeddings are fused with the global layout statistics:
\begin{equation}
h_G = \mathrm{Pool}\left(\mathrm{GNN}(G)\right),
\label{eq:graph_embedding}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_d = \psi_d(d),
\label{eq:design_embedding}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h = \phi([h_G,q,h_d]),
\label{eq:fusion_embedding}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Pool}(\cdot)$ denotes graph-level pooling, $\psi_d(\cdot)$ is the design-parameter encoder, $\phi(\cdot)$ is the fusion network, and $[\cdot]$ denotes feature concatenation.

The two task-specific models use the same representation form but have separate output layers. For the feasibility surrogate, the output layer gives the global feasibility probability:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{f}^{G}=\sigma(f_{\theta_f}(h)),
\label{eq:feasibility_output}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function. For the reinforcement surrogate, the output layer gives the predicted reinforcement usage:
\begin{equation}
\hat{m}_{s}^{G}=f_{\theta_s}(h).
\label{eq:steel_output}
\end{equation}

In training, the feasibility task is optimized using a weighted binary cross-entropy loss to account for class imbalance:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_f = -\omega y_f \log \hat{p}_{f}^{G} - (1-y_f)\log(1-\hat{p}_{f}^{G}),
\label{eq:feasibility_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\omega$ is the class weight. For reinforcement prediction, $m_s$ is transformed using logarithmic scaling and standardization:
\begin{equation}
y_s=\frac{\log(1+m_s)-\mu_s}{\sigma_s},
\label{eq:steel_transform}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_s$ and $\sigma_s$ are computed from the training set. The reinforcement surrogate is trained using the Smooth L1 loss in the transformed space:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_s=\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}\rho_\beta(e_i),
\quad e_i=\hat{y}_{s,i}-y_{s,i},
\label{eq:steel_loss}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\rho_\beta(e_i)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{e_i^2}{2\beta}, & |e_i|<\beta, \\
|e_i|-\dfrac{\beta}{2}, & |e_i|\ge\beta,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:smooth_l1}
\end{equation}
where $N_s$ is the number of reinforcement training samples and $\beta$ controls the transition between the quadratic and linear penalty regions.
The predicted reinforcement usage is recovered by the inverse transformation of Eqn.\ref{eq:steel_transform} during inference.

\subsection{Online Local Calibration of Global Surrogates}
\label{subsec:online_local_calibration}

A global surrogate trained on multiple layouts provides useful prior knowledge for unseen design cases, but it may still exhibit systematic bias for a particular layout or local search region. This issue is especially important in shear wall optimization because the candidate distribution of design variable changes during the search. Therefore, using a static global surrogate throughout the optimization process may lead to inappropriate screening or ranking decisions.

To address this problem, this study introduces online local calibration. During optimization, candidates selected for true evaluation naturally produce FEA-verified samples. These samples are accumulated as a local calibration set:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{loc}
=
\left\{
(z_i,\hat{p}_{f,i}^{G},\hat{m}_{s,i}^{G},y_{f,i},m_{s,i})
\right\}_{i=1}^{n_t},
\label{eq:local_dataset}
\end{equation}
where $t$ denotes the current optimization iteration and $n_t$ is the number of locally verified samples. The local set stores both global surrogate predictions and true FEA-based labels, enabling the calibration module to learn the prediction bias of the global surrogate under the current layout.

\subsubsection{Feasibility probability calibration}
\label{subsubsec:feasibility_calibration}

For feasibility prediction, the goal of calibration is to adjust the global surrogate probability so that it better reflects the observed feasible rate in the current layout and local search region. The global probability is first converted into a logit value:
\begin{equation}
\ell_i^{G}
=
\log
\frac{\hat{p}_{f,i}^{G}}{1-\hat{p}_{f,i}^{G}} .
\label{eq:global_logit}
\end{equation}
When the local calibration set contains enough samples and both feasible and infeasible cases, a lightweight calibration function $g_t(\cdot)$ is fitted:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{f,i}^{C}=g_t(\ell_i^{G}),
\label{eq:calibrated_probability}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{p}_{f,i}^{C}$ is the calibrated feasibility probability.

This calibration adjusts the global feasibility probability $\hat{p}_{f}^{G}$ into a layout-specific feasibility score $\hat{p}_{f}^{C}$. The calibrated score is then used for candidate screening in the current layout. To keep the screening conservative, the threshold is chosen from locally verified feasible samples. Given a target recall $r_0$ for feasible candidates, the raw threshold is defined as
\begin{equation}
\tau_t^{raw}
=
Q_{1-r_0}
\left(
\left\{
\hat{p}_{f,i}^{C}\mid y_{f,i}=1
\right\}
\right),
\label{eq:raw_threshold}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{1-r_0}(\cdot)$ denotes the quantile function. A conservative scaling coefficient $\alpha\in(0,1]$ is then applied:
\begin{equation}
\tau_t^{C}=\alpha \tau_t^{raw}.
\label{eq:calibrated_threshold}
\end{equation}
A candidate with $\hat{p}_{f}^{C}<\tau_t^{C}$ is regarded as low priority for true FEA evaluation. If there are not enough local samples for calibration, the framework falls back to the global feasibility probability and a validation-based global threshold.

\subsubsection{Reinforcement residual calibration}
\label{subsubsec:steel_calibration}

For reinforcement usage, the main purpose of calibration is to correct the systematic residual between the global surrogate prediction and the true reinforcement quantity. For a locally verified sample, the residual is defined as
\begin{equation}
\Delta m_{s,i}
=
m_{s,i}-\hat{m}_{s,i}^{G}.
\label{eq:steel_residual}
\end{equation}
A local residual model $r_t(\cdot)$ is fitted using the local calibration set:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta m}_{s}=r_t(z,\hat{m}_{s}^{G}).
\label{eq:residual_model}
\end{equation}
The calibrated reinforcement prediction is then obtained by
\begin{equation}
\hat{m}_{s}^{C}
=
\hat{m}_{s}^{G}
+
\widehat{\Delta m}_{s}.
\label{eq:calibrated_steel}
\end{equation}

Compared with retraining the full graph surrogate model, this residual calibration strategy is lightweight and sample-efficient. The global surrogate captures the overall mapping learned from multiple layouts, while the local residual model only needs to correct the remaining layout-specific bias. The calibrated reinforcement prediction can be combined with the estimated concrete quantity to form a calibrated surrogate cost:
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}^{C}
=
p_s\hat{m}_{s}^{C}
+
p_c\tilde{m}_{c}.
\label{eq:calibrated_cost}
\end{equation}
This calibrated cost estimate is used for candidate ranking before true evaluation.
