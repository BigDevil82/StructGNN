\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Shear wall structures are widely used in high-rise residential and commercial buildings because they provide high lateral stiffness and load-carrying capacity under wind and seismic actions\cite{Paulay1992SeismicDesign}. In practical engineering design, once the architectural layout and the main shear wall arrangement have been determined, engineers then select suitable section and material parameters, including wall thicknesses, beam dimensions, concrete grades, and other design-related variables. These parameters directly affect structural safety, reinforcement demand, concrete volume, and material cost. However, evaluating a candidate design is not a simple response-prediction task. It usually requires parametric model generation, finite element analysis (FEA), response extraction, component-level reinforcement design, and code-based checking. When population-based optimization algorithms are used, hundreds or thousands of candidate designs may be generated. Repeated FEA and code checking therefore become the main computational bottleneck in the section and material optimization of shear wall structures.

Considerable efforts have been made to improve the automation and optimization of shear wall structural design. Early optimization studies formulated the design of reinforced concrete shear wall or wall-slab systems as cost minimization problems and used genetic algorithms to search for economical section or material configurations \cite{Atabay2009}. Recent studies have further extended shear wall optimization to seismic risk-oriented design, showing that the configuration of shear walls in reinforced concrete framed buildings can be adjusted to reduce seismic risk and improve structural performance \cite{cere2022shear}. Tafraout et al. combined genetic optimization with an automatic design workflow in a BIM environment for reinforced concrete wall-slab buildings \cite{Tafraout2019}, and similar BIM-based automation has been extended to the optimization of reinforced concrete buildings \cite{bourahla2023intelligent, li2021dfma}. For the layout design problem, Zhang and Mueller investigated shear wall layout optimization at the conceptual design stage \cite{Zhang2017}, while Lou et al. proposed a data-driven tabu search strategy for high-rise building layouts \cite{Lou2021}. Other studies have incorporated engineering prior knowledge and modified evolutionary algorithms to improve the practicality of automated shear wall design \cite{Zhou2022}. These studies demonstrate the value of optimization algorithms and automatic modeling tools, but their evaluation process still relies heavily on structural analysis and design checking. As a result, the computational cost of candidate evaluation remains a major obstacle when a large number of section-material alternatives must be explored for a fixed layout.

Data-driven generative design has recently provided another route for intelligent shear wall design. Liao et al. proposed a GAN-based method that learns shear wall design patterns from engineering drawings and generates wall layouts from architectural conditions \cite{Liao2021}. Lu et al. improved this direction by introducing physical information into the generative process, so that generated shear wall schemes can better satisfy structural-performance requirements \cite{Lu2022}. Fei et al. developed an integrated schematic design method to make GAN-based shear wall design more applicable in practical workflows \cite{Fei2022}. To address the limited and imbalanced nature of engineering design data, Fei et al. further incorporated structural optimization into a semi-supervised learning framework for shear-wall structure design using small and long-tailed datasets \cite{fei2023semi}. Zhao et al. introduced graph neural network (GNN) to represent shear wall layouts, reducing the dependence on pixel-based image representations and better capturing topological relationships \cite{Zhao2023}. Diffusion-based methods have also been explored for end-to-end shear wall layout generation and analysis \cite{Leng2024}. More recently, large language models and generative artificial intelligence have been integrated into intelligent structural design assistants, such as AIstructure-Copilot, indicating a broader trend toward AI-assisted building structural design workflows \cite{qin2024aistructure}. These studies mainly address layout generation or schematic structural design. In contrast, the present study focuses on a different stage: for a given shear wall layout, how to optimize section and material parameters efficiently while retaining high-fidelity FEA and code checking as the final verification basis.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has also attracted increasing attention in structural design optimization because it can learn sequential design policies from interaction with an analysis environment. Jeong and Jo proposed a deep RL framework for automated design of reinforced concrete structures \cite{Jeong2021}. Hayashi and Ohsaki combined RL with graph embedding for binary truss topology optimization under stress and displacement constraints \cite{Hayashi2020}. Fu et al. developed a physics-informed deep RL framework, FrameRL, for automated steel frame structure design \cite{Fu2024}, while Du et al. introduced FrameGym as a reusable RL environment for steel frame structures \cite{Du2025FrameGym}. To further improve the scalability of RL-based structural optimization, Du et al. developed MAFO-3D, a multi-agent RL framework for efficient optimization of three-dimensional steel moment-resisting frames \cite{Du2025MAFO3D}. Li et al. also applied deep RL to the design optimization of suspended ceiling systems in space grid structures, showing the potential of RL for complex structural component systems \cite{Li2026CeilingRL}. More recently, RL has also been applied to automated design of shear wall structures with code compliance \cite{Zhao2026}. These studies show the potential of RL in learning design strategies for structural systems. However, RL-based structural optimization usually requires many interactions between the agent and the structural evaluation environment. This requirement is particularly restrictive for shear wall structures because large-scale random generation of realistic layouts is difficult, engineering design drawings are costly to collect, and each candidate evaluation involves time-consuming FEA and code-based reinforcement design. Therefore, for the problem considered in this paper, directly training an RL policy over a large number of shear wall design cases may introduce a computational and data-collection burden comparable to, or even larger than, the original optimization problem.

Surrogate-assisted optimization provides a more direct way to reduce the cost of expensive simulation-based design. Classical surrogate modeling and response-surface-based optimization methods, including Kriging, radial basis functions, support vector machines, and neural networks, have been widely used to approximate expensive objective or constraint functions \cite{Jones1998}. Queipo et al. reviewed surrogate-based analysis and optimization for expensive engineering simulations \cite{Queipo2005}, and Forrester and Keane summarized later advances in surrogate-based optimization \cite{Forrester2009}. In structural engineering, surrogate models have been increasingly used to approximate finite-element responses, support reliability analysis, and accelerate design exploration \cite{Kudela2022,kong2024rapid}. Samadian et al. also reviewed data-driven surrogate models in structural engineering and emphasized their role in reducing computational burden \cite{Samadian2024}. These studies provide a clear motivation for using surrogate models in FEA-intensive structural optimization. Nevertheless, in shear wall section-material optimization, the surrogate is not expected to fully replace the true evaluator. The more practical question is whether fast predictions can help decide which candidates should be screened out, which should be ranked as promising, and which should be submitted to FEA under a limited evaluation budget.

However, ordinary global surrogate accuracy is insufficient for candidate assessment in shear wall section-material optimization. For the same wall thicknesses, beam sections, and material grades, different shear wall layouts may lead to different stiffness distributions, load paths, torsional responses, and member force patterns. This observation is consistent with previous layout-generation and graph-based shear wall design studies, where spatial organization and topology are treated as important design information \cite{Zhao2023}. Therefore, a surrogate model that only uses section and material parameters cannot reliably support cross-layout screening and ranking. A layout-conditioned representation is needed to estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand while explicitly accounting for the structural configuration.

In addition, the surrogate outputs used in optimization should be reliable for their intended decisions rather than merely accurate in an average sense. Feasibility prediction is mainly used for conservative screening, where falsely rejecting a feasible candidate may remove a potential optimum before FEA verification; reinforcement prediction is mainly used for cost-oriented ranking, where relative ordering is more important than exact quantity prediction. Moreover, the candidate distribution changes during optimization as the search gradually concentrates on a local region of the design space for the current layout. A global surrogate trained on many layouts may therefore exhibit layout-specific or region-specific bias. Since every FEA-evaluated candidate naturally provides verified feasibility and reinforcement information, these samples can be used to calibrate feasibility probabilities and correct reinforcement residuals online, instead of retraining the full surrogate model.

Based on the above considerations, this study proposes a calibrated surrogate-assisted optimization framework for shear wall structural design with improved FEA efficiency.
For a given shear wall layout, the framework first uses global surrogate models to quickly estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand, so that clearly low-value candidates can be filtered and more promising candidates can be prioritized. The selected candidates are then submitted to high-fidelity FEA and code-based checking, and the verified results are reused to calibrate the surrogate predictions for the current layout during optimization. In this way, the framework reallocates the limited FEA budget toward candidates that are more likely to be feasible and economical, while the final best design is always selected from FEA-verified feasible candidates.

The main innovations and contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[(\arabic*)]
\item A layout-conditioned surrogate modeling strategy is developed for shear wall section-material optimization. It enables candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand to be estimated before expensive FEA while explicitly incorporating layout information.
\item An online local calibration mechanism is introduced to adapt global surrogate predictions to the current layout. FEA-verified samples generated during optimization are reused to calibrate feasibility probabilities and correct reinforcement residuals without retraining the full surrogate model.
\item A calibrated surrogate-assisted candidate assessment strategy is established for FEA budget allocation. By combining conservative feasibility screening with cost-oriented priority ranking, the framework reduces unnecessary FEA calls while keeping high-fidelity FEA and code checking as the final verification basis.
\end{enumerate}
