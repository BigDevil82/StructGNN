\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Shear wall systems are widely used in high-rise buildings to resist lateral loads~\cite{Paulay1992SeismicDesign}. Once a structural layout is determined, engineers must size wall thicknesses, beam dimensions, and concrete grades. Identifying an economical design requires repeated structural modeling, finite element analysis (FEA), reinforcement design, and code-based checking. When population-based optimization algorithms explore hundreds of candidate schemes for a given layout, these repeated evaluations become the primary computational bottleneck.

Recent advances in intelligent structural design have automated the generation of shear wall layouts. Early studies adopted generative adversarial networks to learn wall arrangements from architectural drawings~\cite{Liao2021} and incorporated physical rules or integrated workflows to enhance layout quality~\cite{Lu2022,Fei2022}. More recent work has explored graph neural networks to represent room-wall topology~\cite{Zhao2023} and diffusion models for end-to-end layout synthesis~\cite{Leng2024,ArchiDiffusion}. However, these generative methods focus on planar layout synthesis. The resulting schemes still require downstream section sizing, material selection, and code verification. This study addresses this subsequent optimization stage for a prescribed wall and beam layout.

Existing approaches to automated structural sizing primarily rely on metaheuristic search or reinforcement learning. Evolutionary algorithms have been combined with automated BIM modeling~\cite{Atabay2009,Tafraout2019} and engineering prior knowledge~\cite{Zhou2022} to optimize member dimensions, while reinforcement learning has been applied to frame sizing~\cite{Jeong2021,Du2025FrameGym} and code-compliant shear wall design~\cite{Zhao2026}. Both paradigms require extensive structural evaluations during either iterative search or policy training. Although deep learning models can accelerate structural response prediction~\cite{djerrad2024efficient,zhou2024physics}, predicting detailed response histories does not directly yield optimization decisions, which depend on overall code compliance and material cost.

Surrogate-based optimization offers a more direct way to reduce evaluation costs by using fast approximations to guide expensive simulations~\cite{Jones1998,Forrester2009}. In structural engineering, surrogate models have been increasingly adopted to approximate finite element responses and accelerate design exploration~\cite{Kudela2022,Samadian2024}. Rather than replacing the true evaluator, a practical surrogate in shear wall sizing should assess whether a candidate is likely to pass code checks and whether its material cost is competitive prior to FEA. Combining both signals enables selective allocation of the limited FEA budget to promising designs.

Two challenges arise when applying surrogate-informed optimization across diverse shear wall buildings. First, identical section and material parameters can produce markedly different stiffness distributions, internal forces, and reinforcement demands depending on the wall layout. A surrogate must therefore condition its predictions on structural topology as well as design variables. Second, a global surrogate trained across layouts can exhibit systematic bias on an unseen building, particularly as the search narrows into a local region of the design space. Newly evaluated designs during optimization provide direct feedback to correct such layout-specific bias online.

To address these challenges, this study proposes a calibrated surrogate-informed optimization framework (CASIO) for FEA-efficient shear wall section and material design. Specifically, two independently trained layout-conditioned models (FuseGNN) combine room-adjacency graphs, global layout statistics, and design parameters to predict candidate feasibility and reinforcement mass. During optimization, FEA-verified samples collected from the current building update lightweight local calibration models, which adjust feasibility probabilities and correct reinforcement prediction residuals without retraining the global networks. The calibrated predictions then guide conservative feasibility screening and cost-oriented ranking to prioritize candidates for FEA, while final feasibility and cost remain strictly determined by the true structural evaluator.

The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A \textbf{layout-conditioned surrogate model} with a graph-tabular fusion architecture (FuseGNN) is developed to predict structural feasibility and reinforcement demand across varying shear wall layouts prior to FEA.
    \item A lightweight \textbf{online local calibration} mechanism uses FEA-verified samples from the ongoing optimization to calibrate feasibility scores and correct reinforcement residuals without retraining the global networks.
    \item A unified screening-and-ranking evaluation interface enables \textbf{decision-oriented FEA allocation} to concentrate limited FEA calls on feasible, low-cost candidates across different search algorithms.
\end{itemize}
