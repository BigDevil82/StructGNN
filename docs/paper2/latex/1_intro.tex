\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Shear wall structures are widely used in high-rise residential and commercial buildings because they provide high lateral stiffness and load-carrying capacity under wind and seismic actions. In practical engineering design, once the architectural layout and the main shear wall arrangement have been determined, engineers still need to select suitable section and material parameters, including wall thicknesses, beam dimensions, concrete grades, and other design-related variables. These parameters directly affect structural safety, reinforcement demand, concrete volume, and material cost. However, evaluating a candidate design is not a simple response-prediction task. It usually requires parametric model generation, finite element analysis (FEA), response extraction, member-level reinforcement design, and code-based checking. When population-based optimization algorithms are used, hundreds or thousands of candidate designs may be generated. Repeated FEA and code checking therefore become the main computational bottleneck in section and material optimization of shear wall structures.

Considerable efforts have been made to improve the automation and optimization of shear wall structural design. Early optimization studies formulated the design of reinforced concrete shear wall or wall-slab systems as cost minimization problems and used genetic algorithms to search for economical section or material configurations \cite{Atabay2009}. Tafraout et al. further combined genetic optimization with an automatic design workflow in a BIM environment for reinforced concrete wall-slab buildings \cite{Tafraout2019}. For the layout design problem, Zhang and Mueller investigated shear wall layout optimization at the conceptual design stage \cite{Zhang2017}, while Lou et al. proposed a data-driven tabu search strategy for high-rise building layouts \cite{Lou2021}. Other studies have incorporated engineering prior knowledge and modified evolutionary algorithms to improve the practicality of automated shear wall design \cite{Zhou2022}. These studies demonstrate the value of optimization algorithms and automatic modeling tools, but their evaluation process still relies heavily on structural analysis and design checking. As a result, the computational cost of candidate evaluation remains a major obstacle when a large number of section-material alternatives must be explored for a fixed layout.

Data-driven generative design has recently provided another route for intelligent shear wall design. Liao et al. proposed a GAN-based method that learns shear wall design patterns from engineering drawings and generates wall layouts from architectural conditions \cite{Liao2021}. Lu et al. improved this direction by introducing physical information into the generative process, so that generated shear wall schemes can better satisfy structural-performance requirements \cite{Lu2022}. Fei et al. developed an integrated schematic design method to make GAN-based shear wall design more applicable in practical workflows \cite{Fei2022}. Zhao et al. introduced graph neural networks to represent shear wall layouts, reducing the dependence on pixel-based image representations and better capturing topological relationships \cite{Zhao2023}. Diffusion-based methods have also been explored for end-to-end shear wall layout generation and analysis \cite{Leng2024}. These studies mainly address layout generation or schematic structural design. In contrast, the present study focuses on a different stage: for a given shear wall layout, how to optimize section and material parameters efficiently while retaining high-fidelity FEA and code checking as the final verification basis.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has also attracted increasing attention in structural design optimization because it can learn sequential design policies from interaction with an analysis environment. Jeong and Jo proposed a deep RL framework for automated design of reinforced concrete structures \cite{Jeong2021}. Hayashi and Ohsaki combined RL with graph embedding for binary truss topology optimization under stress and displacement constraints \cite{Hayashi2020}. Fu et al. developed a physics-informed deep RL framework, FrameRL, for automated steel frame structure design \cite{Fu2024}. Rochefort-Beaudoin et al. introduced SOgym as an open-source RL environment for structural topology optimization \cite{RochefortBeaudoin2025}. More recently, RL has also been applied to automated design of shear wall structures with code compliance \cite{Zhao2026}. These studies show the potential of RL in learning design strategies for structural systems. However, RL-based structural optimization usually requires many interactions between the agent and the structural evaluation environment. This requirement is particularly restrictive for shear wall structures because large-scale random generation of realistic layouts is difficult, engineering design drawings are costly to collect, and each candidate evaluation involves time-consuming FEA and code-based reinforcement design. Therefore, for the problem considered in this paper, directly training an RL policy over a large number of shear wall design cases may introduce a computational and data-collection burden comparable to, or even larger than, the original optimization problem.

Surrogate-assisted optimization provides a more direct way to reduce the cost of expensive simulation-based design. Classical surrogate modeling and response-surface-based optimization methods, including Kriging, radial basis functions, support vector machines, and neural networks, have been widely used to approximate expensive objective or constraint functions \cite{Jones1998}. Queipo et al. reviewed surrogate-based analysis and optimization for expensive engineering simulations \cite{Queipo2005}, and Forrester and Keane summarized later advances in surrogate-based optimization \cite{Forrester2009}. In structural engineering, surrogate models have been increasingly used to approximate finite-element responses, support reliability analysis, and accelerate design exploration \cite{Kudela2022}. Samadian et al. also reviewed data-driven surrogate models in structural engineering and emphasized their role in reducing computational burden \cite{Samadian2024}. These studies provide a clear motivation for using surrogate models in FEA-intensive structural optimization. Nevertheless, in shear wall section-material optimization, the surrogate is not expected to fully replace the true evaluator. The more practical question is whether fast predictions can help decide which candidates should be screened out, which should be ranked as promising, and which should be submitted to FEA under a limited evaluation budget.

This candidate-assessment perspective leads to two requirements that are not fully captured by ordinary global prediction accuracy. First, the prediction model should account for the structural layout. For the same wall thicknesses, beam sections, and material grades, different shear wall arrangements may produce different stiffness distributions, load paths, torsional responses, and member force patterns. This observation is consistent with previous layout-generation and graph-based shear wall design studies, where spatial organization and topology are treated as important design information \cite{Zhao2023}. Therefore, a surrogate model that only uses section and material parameters is insufficient for cross-layout candidate assessment. A layout-conditioned representation is needed so that the surrogate can distinguish different structural configurations while evaluating the same type of design variables.

Second, the surrogate outputs used for optimization should be reliable for their intended decisions. Feasibility prediction is used for conservative screening, where falsely rejecting a feasible candidate may remove a potential optimum before FEA verification. Thus, probability reliability and feasible-candidate recall are more important than ordinary classification accuracy alone. This is related to the broader machine-learning literature showing that accurate classifiers may still produce poorly calibrated probabilities \cite{Guo2017}. Calibration methods such as Platt scaling and isotonic regression have been widely studied to improve the consistency between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies \cite{Platt1999}. Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana also showed that different supervised learning algorithms can have substantially different probability-calibration behavior \cite{NiculescuMizil2005}. In contrast, reinforcement usage prediction is mainly used for cost-oriented ranking. For this task, the relative order of candidates may be more important than the exact reinforcement quantity. These different error costs suggest that feasibility screening and reinforcement-based ranking should be treated as complementary functions rather than merged into a single surrogate score.

Another practical issue is that the candidate distribution changes during optimization. A global surrogate trained on samples from many layouts can provide useful prior knowledge, but the optimizer gradually concentrates on a local region of the section-material design space for the current layout. The errors of the global surrogate in this local region may directly affect screening and ranking decisions. At the same time, every candidate submitted to FEA naturally produces verified information, including the true feasibility label and reinforcement usage. These verified samples provide an opportunity to correct the bias of the global surrogate for the current layout and search region. Instead of retraining the full surrogate model, a lightweight local calibration module can use these samples to adjust feasibility probabilities and reinforcement residuals during optimization.

Based on the above considerations, this paper proposes a calibrated surrogate-assisted optimization framework for FEA-efficient shear wall structural design. The study focuses on section and material parameter optimization for a given shear wall layout. A layout-conditioned graph surrogate model is first trained to estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement usage from room-level graph representations, global layout statistics, and design parameters. During optimization, FEA-verified samples accumulated from the current layout are stored in a local calibration buffer. These samples are used to calibrate the feasibility probability and correct the reinforcement residual of the global surrogate. The calibrated feasibility prediction is then used for conservative candidate screening, while the calibrated reinforcement prediction is used for cost-oriented priority ranking. Only selected candidates are submitted to high-fidelity FEA and code-based checking, and the final best design is always selected from FEA-verified feasible candidates.

The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows. First, a layout-conditioned surrogate modeling strategy is developed for shear wall section-material optimization, enabling candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand to be estimated before expensive FEA while explicitly considering layout information. Second, an online local calibration mechanism is introduced to correct layout-specific probability and residual bias using FEA-verified samples generated during the optimization process. Third, a calibrated surrogate-assisted candidate assessment strategy is established to allocate limited FEA budgets through conservative feasibility screening and cost-oriented priority ranking, thereby improving optimization efficiency while retaining high-fidelity verification as the final decision basis.
