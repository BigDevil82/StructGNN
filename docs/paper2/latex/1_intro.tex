\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Shear wall structures are widely used in high-rise residential and commercial buildings because they provide high lateral stiffness and load-carrying capacity under wind and seismic actions\cite{Paulay1992SeismicDesign}. In practical engineering design, once the architectural layout and the main shear wall arrangement have been determined, engineers then select suitable section and material parameters, including wall thicknesses, beam dimensions, concrete grades, and other design-related variables. These parameters directly affect structural safety, reinforcement demand, concrete volume, and material cost. However, evaluating a candidate design is not a simple response-prediction task. It usually requires parametric model generation, finite element analysis (FEA), response extraction, component-level reinforcement design, and code-based checking. When population-based optimization algorithms are used, hundreds or thousands of candidate designs may be generated. Repeated FEA and code checking therefore become the main computational bottleneck in the section and material optimization of shear wall structures.

Data-driven generative design has advanced the automation of shear wall structural design. GAN-based studies have generated shear wall layouts from architectural conditions and incorporated physical information, integrated workflows, and semi-supervised learning to improve structural performance and address limited engineering data \cite{Liao2021,Lu2022,Fei2022,fei2023semi}. More recent studies have used graph neural networks to represent layout topology \cite{Zhao2023}, diffusion models for end-to-end layout generation and analysis \cite{Leng2024,ArchiDiffusion}, and large language models to support intelligent structural design workflows \cite{qin2024aistructure}. These developments enable rapid generation and representation of structural schemes, but the resulting layouts still require structural design and verification before engineering application. This study therefore focuses on the downstream optimization stage, aiming to improve the efficiency of section and material design for a given layout.

Considerable efforts have been made to improve the automation and optimization of shear wall structural design. Early optimization studies formulated the design of reinforced concrete shear wall or wall-slab systems as cost minimization problems and used genetic algorithms to search for economical section or material configurations \cite{Atabay2009}. Recent studies have further extended shear wall optimization to seismic risk-oriented design, showing that the configuration of shear walls in reinforced concrete framed buildings can be adjusted to reduce seismic risk and improve structural performance \cite{cere2022shear}. Tafraout et al. combined genetic optimization with an automatic design workflow in a BIM environment for reinforced concrete wall-slab buildings \cite{Tafraout2019}, and similar BIM-based automation has been extended to the optimization of reinforced concrete buildings \cite{bourahla2023intelligent, li2021dfma}. For the layout design problem, Zhang and Mueller investigated shear wall layout optimization at the conceptual design stage \cite{Zhang2017}, while Lou et al. proposed a data-driven tabu search strategy for high-rise building layouts \cite{Lou2021}. Other studies have incorporated engineering prior knowledge and modified evolutionary algorithms to improve the practicality of automated shear wall design \cite{Zhou2022}. These studies demonstrate the value of optimization algorithms and automatic modeling tools, but their evaluation process still relies heavily on structural analysis and design checking. As a result, the computational cost of candidate evaluation remains a major obstacle when a large number of section-material alternatives must be explored for a fixed layout.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has also been widely explored for structural design optimization. Representative applications include automated reinforced-concrete design \cite{Jeong2021}, graph-based truss topology optimization \cite{Hayashi2020}, physics-informed steel-frame design \cite{Fu2024}, reusable RL environments and multi-agent optimization for three-dimensional frames \cite{Du2025FrameGym,Du2025MAFO3D}, suspended-ceiling optimization \cite{Li2026CeilingRL}, and code-compliant shear wall design \cite{Zhao2026}. These studies demonstrate the ability of RL to learn effective design policies through interaction with structural analysis environments. However, RL training generally requires a large number of structural evaluations. This is particularly costly for shear wall structures, where realistic layouts are difficult to generate at scale, engineering drawings are limited, and each evaluation involves time-consuming FEA, reinforcement design, and code checking. Consequently, training a general RL policy across many layouts may require computational and data resources comparable to, or greater than, those of the original optimization problem.

Deep learning-based structural response prediction provides another potential approach to reducing repeated structural analyses. Djerrad et al. developed efficient deep learning models for rapidly predicting the responses of RC shear walls under monotonic and cyclic loading \cite{djerrad2024efficient}. Zhou et al.  and Meng et al. further proposed real-time seismic response prediction methods  based on physics-informed deep learning and a refined self-attention mechanism, respectively \cite{zhou2024physics,meng2024refined}. These studies demonstrate that data-driven models can reproduce detailed structural response curves or time histories at substantially lower computational cost than conventional numerical  analysis. However, predicting complete response sequences remains a high-dimensional and data-intensive task, and the resulting outputs are not directly aligned with structural optimization decisions. Candidate evaluation  still requires the synthesis of global responses, component-level demands, code-compliance checks, reinforcement design, and material cost.

Surrogate-informed optimization provides a more direct way to reduce the cost of expensive simulation-based design. Classical surrogate modeling and response-surface-based optimization methods, including Kriging, radial basis functions, support vector machines, and neural networks, have been widely used to approximate expensive objective or constraint functions \cite{Jones1998}. Queipo et al. reviewed surrogate-based analysis and optimization for expensive engineering simulations \cite{Queipo2005}, and Forrester and Keane summarized later advances in surrogate-based optimization \cite{Forrester2009}. In structural engineering, surrogate models have been increasingly used to approximate finite-element responses, support reliability analysis, and accelerate design exploration \cite{Kudela2022,kong2024rapid}. Samadian et al. also reviewed data-driven surrogate models in structural engineering and emphasized their role in reducing computational burden \cite{Samadian2024}. These studies provide a clear motivation for using surrogate models in FEA-intensive structural optimization. Nevertheless, in shear wall section-material optimization, the surrogate is not expected to fully replace the true evaluator. The more practical question is whether fast predictions can help decide which candidates should be screened out, which should be ranked as promising, and which should be submitted to FEA under a limited evaluation budget.

However, ordinary global surrogate accuracy is insufficient for candidate assessment in shear wall section-material optimization. For the same wall thicknesses, beam sections, and material grades, different shear wall layouts may lead to different stiffness distributions, load paths, torsional responses, and member force patterns. This observation is consistent with previous layout-generation and graph-based shear wall design studies, where spatial organization and topology are treated as important design information \cite{Zhao2023}. Therefore, a surrogate model that only uses section and material parameters cannot reliably support cross-layout screening and ranking. A layout-conditioned representation is needed to estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand while explicitly accounting for the structural configuration.

In addition, the surrogate outputs used in optimization should be reliable for their intended decisions rather than merely accurate in an average sense. Feasibility prediction is mainly used for conservative screening, where falsely rejecting a feasible candidate may remove a potential optimum before FEA verification; reinforcement prediction is mainly used for cost-oriented ranking, where relative ordering is more important than exact quantity prediction. Moreover, the candidate distribution changes during optimization as the search gradually concentrates on a local region of the design space for the current layout. A global surrogate trained on many layouts may therefore exhibit layout-specific or region-specific bias. Since every FEA-evaluated candidate naturally provides verified feasibility and reinforcement information, these samples can be used to calibrate feasibility probabilities and correct reinforcement residuals online, instead of retraining the full surrogate model.

Based on the above considerations, this study proposes a calibrated surrogate-informed optimization framework (CASINO) for shear wall structural design with improved FEA efficiency.
For a given shear wall layout, the framework first uses global surrogate models to quickly estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand, so that clearly low-value candidates can be filtered and more competitive candidates can be prioritized. The selected candidates are then submitted to FEA and code-based checking, and the verified results are reused to calibrate the surrogate predictions for the current layout during optimization. In this way, the framework reallocates the limited FEA budget toward candidates that are more likely to be feasible and economical, while the final best design is always selected from FEA-verified feasible candidates.

The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[(\arabic*)]
\item A layout-conditioned surrogate model named FuseGNN is proposed for shear wall section-material optimization, which incorporates layout information to estimate candidate feasibility and reinforcement demand prior to FEA.
\item An online local calibration mechanism is introduced to adapt global surrogate predictions to the current layout, using FEA-verified samples to calibrate feasibility probabilities and correct reinforcement residuals.
\item A screening and ranking strategy is developed to allocate FEA evaluations by filtering low-feasibility candidates and selecting low-cost ones.
\end{enumerate}
