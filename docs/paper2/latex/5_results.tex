\subsection{Global Surrogate Model Performance}
\label{subsec:global_surrogate_results}

Tables~\ref{tab:global_feasibility_results} and~\ref{tab:global_steel_results} compare the cross-layout generalization of the global surrogates across progressively enriched representations. When relying solely on design parameters, Param-MLP performs poorly on unseen layouts (feasibility PR-AUC of 0.6137 and reinforcement $R^2$ of 0.351), confirming that section dimensions alone cannot capture layout-dependent stiffness and load-path variations. Incorporating seven room-graph summaries (Stat-MLP) substantially improves both tasks, while FuseGNN—combining message passing over the room-adjacency graph with global layout statistics—achieves the strongest performance (PR-AUC of 0.8593, F1 of 0.7688, reinforcement MAE of 11.48~t, and $R^2$ of 0.924).

More importantly, FuseGNN aligns well with decision-oriented screening and ranking. At the validation-selected operating threshold, it filters out 47.36\% of candidate designs prior to FEA while retaining 99.98\% of truly feasible solutions (only three false rejections across the test set). For reinforcement prediction, it achieves a pairwise ranking accuracy of 0.9787 and a top-10\% recall of 0.9483, reliably identifying economical candidates within each layout.

Fig.~\ref{fig:global_surrogate_overview} further diagnoses the error structure of FuseGNN. While the global model captures overall trends (Fig.~\ref{fig:global_surrogate_overview}(a) and (c)), layout-wise probability comparisons (Fig.~\ref{fig:global_surrogate_overview}(b)) reveal over-optimistic feasibility predictions on several low-feasibility layouts. Similarly, reinforcement errors grow in high-demand regimes (Fig.~\ref{fig:global_surrogate_overview}(d)), reaching an MAE of 20.7~t and a bias of $-17.4$~t in the top 20\% usage quantile. These systematic layout- and demand-dependent biases directly motivate online local adaptation during optimization.

Using 100 randomly sampled local FEA records per layout, Fig.~\ref{fig:local_calibration_effect} illustrates how local adaptation corrects layout-specific prediction errors across all test layouts, with the largest gains occurring on layouts where the uncalibrated global model exhibits severe initial bias.

Table~\ref{tab:local_calibration_results} compares calibrating the global surrogate against training a local model from scratch (`Local only'). Without the global prior, `Local only' suffers a sharp drop in feasibility recall (from 0.9990 to 0.7265) and yields a reinforcement MAE of 4.82~t. By contrast, local calibration retains cross-layout structural knowledge while adapting to the current layout. Platt scaling lowers the feasibility Brier score from 0.0749 to 0.0473 and ECE from 0.0895 to 0.0302 while preserving a 0.9942 recall, and residual Gaussian process regression reduces reinforcement MAE by 82.2\% (from 11.48~t to 2.04~t, $R^2 = 0.994$).

Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration_sample_efficiency} tracks calibration performance from 25 to 500 local samples (detailed values in \ref{app:calibration_results}). Error reductions grow steadily with sample count, and the optimal reinforcement residual model scales with data availability. Ridge regression performs best at 25 samples, Gaussian process regression at 50--100 samples, and LightGBM at 200--500 samples. For feasibility, Platt scaling already reduces the Brier score by 30.5\% at 25 samples and stays close to the best residual random forest up to 100 samples before plateauing at larger counts (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration_sample_efficiency}(a)--(b)); it is therefore deployed online for its simplicity and resistance to small-sample overfitting.

Table~\ref{tab:optimization_summary_results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:optimization_benchmark_results} summarize the optimization benchmarks across 15 unseen layouts and five seeds. Across all three optimizers, surrogate-informed evaluation substantially reduces true FEA demand. Compared with Full FEA, `Cost ranking' reduces FEA calls by 50.1\%--57.0\%, while adding feasibility screening (`Screen \& ranking') increases FEA savings to 65.7\% in GA, 62.4\% in PSO, and 65.6\% in random search—averaging a 64.6\% reduction across all 225 runs. Simultaneously, both surrogate-informed strategies accelerate feasible-region discovery compared with Full FEA, reducing the mean FEA calls to the first feasible solution from 72 to 24 `Cost ranking' and 28 `Screen \& ranking' in GA, from 105 to 56 and 51 in PSO, and from 71 to 42 and 31 in random search.

This computational speedup is achieved with minimal impact on design quality and discovery reliability. `Screen \& ranking' achieves feasible-solution discovery rates comparable to Full FEA (e.g., 65.3\% vs.\ 68.0\% in GA, and 66.7\% vs.\ 68.0\% in random search). Across all 142 paired runs where both `Screen \& ranking' and Full FEA find feasible designs, the mean material cost increase is only 1.19\%, with a median of approximately 0\% and a worst-case increase of 17.39\%.

Fig.~\ref{fig:cost_tolerance_analysis} further evaluates the trade-off between cost optimality and FEA savings by plotting the fraction of runs that find a feasible design with fewer FEA calls within a specified cost tolerance of the Full FEA optimum. As the tolerance increases from 0\% to 5\%, the qualified ratio rises sharply and plateaus near the full-FEA discovery ceiling across all three optimizers. These results confirm that CASIO operates effectively as an optimizer-agnostic evaluation interface.

\label{subsec:mechanism_discussion}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering

\caption{Feasible-solution discovery patterns across test layouts and optimization strategies.}
\label{fig:layout_difficulty_heatmap}
\end{figure*}

To examine how CASIO allocates evaluations during search, Fig.~\ref{fig:candidate_flow_analysis} tracks the generation-wise status of generated candidates. In GA and PSO, roughly 20\% of candidates are filtered out by feasibility screening and another 40\% are skipped by cost-oriented ranking, leaving approximately 40\% evaluated or cached (with ~20\% of all candidates confirmed feasible). In random search, the corresponding fractions are roughly 35\% rejected, 30\% skipped, and 33\% evaluated or cached. Retaining some borderline infeasible candidates reflects the intentionally conservative screening threshold designed to prevent false rejection of valid optima.

Fig.~\ref{fig:optimization_case_examples} compares search trajectories on three representative layouts of increasing difficulty (decreasing feasible-region ratio). In the `Easy' and `Medium' layouts, `Cost ranking' alone concentrates evaluations in low-cost regions but still wastes FEA calls on cheap, infeasible designs. Adding feasibility screening (`Screen \& ranking') filters out these violations and converges to comparable optimal costs with far fewer evaluations. The `Hard' layout demonstrates why feasibility screening is indispensable. Because feasible designs require thicker sections and higher material costs, `Cost ranking' alone remains trapped in the low-cost infeasible region and fails to locate any valid solution. By rejecting low-feasibility candidates, `Screen \& ranking' steers the search toward thicker, code-compliant configurations and successfully identifies an economical feasible design with less than 40\% of the baseline FEA budget.

Finally, the layout-by-layout success heatmap in Fig.~\ref{fig:layout_difficulty_heatmap} clarifies the practical scope of the framework. Layouts that fail consistently under `Screen \& ranking' (e.g., Group8-5) also fail in nearly all Full FEA runs, reflecting inherent geometric or design-space constraints rather than aggressive screening alone, although screening occasionally misses feasible runs on hard layouts (e.g., Group8-1 in GA). Consequently, CASIO is most advantageous for practical engineering problems where feasible solutions exist within the sizing space, but exhaustive FEA exploration is computationally prohibitive.
