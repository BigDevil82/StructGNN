\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study proposed CASIO, a calibrated surrogate-informed optimization framework for FEA-efficient section and material design of shear wall structures. By combining a layout-conditioned graph surrogate (FuseGNN) with online local calibration and feasibility-aware cost ranking, CASIO concentrates limited structural evaluations on competitive candidate designs. Based on experiments across 143 real-world shear wall layouts, the main conclusions are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Incorporating room-adjacency graphs and global layout statistics enables FuseGNN to capture layout-dependent structural behavior, achieving a feasibility PR-AUC of 0.8593 and a reinforcement prediction $R^2$ of 0.924 on unseen layouts, which substantially outperforms parameter-only and summary-only baselines.

\item Adapting frozen global predictions using local FEA-verified samples effectively mitigates layout-specific systematic bias. In offline evaluations with 100 randomly sampled records per layout, Platt scaling lowers the feasibility Brier score from 0.0749 to 0.0473 and ECE from 0.0895 to 0.0302 at a 0.9942 feasible recall, while residual Gaussian process regression reduces reinforcement MAE by 82.2\% (from 11.48~t to 2.04~t).

\item Across 15 unseen test layouts and three search algorithms (GA, PSO, and random search), the combined screening-and-ranking strategy reduces true FEA calls by an average of 64.6\% while accelerating first-feasible discovery, incurring a mean material cost increase of only 1.19\% (with a median near 0\%) across 142 paired feasible runs.

\item Reinforcement prediction and feasibility screening play complementary roles across layouts of varying difficulty. The former steers the search away from costly designs, while the latter prevents optimizers from being trapped in low-cost infeasible regions on challenging layouts.
\end{enumerate}

This work focuses on section and material sizing for prescribed shear wall layouts under the implemented Chinese code checks, where local adaptation relies on search-selected samples without matched-budget or calibration-only online ablations. Future work will incorporate uncertainty-aware adaptive screening, matched-budget ablation analyses, and joint optimization of shear wall layout topology and member sizing.
