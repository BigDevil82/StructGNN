\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study proposed a calibrated surrogate-assisted optimization framework for cross-sectional and material optimization of shear wall structures. The proposed framework uses surrogate models as screening and ranking tools rather than direct substitutes for FEA. A room-graph-based LayoutParamGNN was developed to predict structural feasibility and reinforcement usage, while online local calibration was introduced to correct layout-specific prediction bias using FEA-verified samples accumulated during optimization. The final design solution is still determined by true FEA and code checking, thereby preserving the reliability requirement of engineering design.

The experimental results lead to the following conclusions.

First, layout information is essential for surrogate modeling in shear wall structural optimization. Compared with models using only design parameters or global layout statistics, the room-graph LayoutParamGNN achieved better performance in both feasibility screening and reinforcement usage prediction. On the test layouts, the feasibility surrogate achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.9693 and a PR-AUC of 0.8593, while the reinforcement surrogate achieved an $R^2$ of 0.924 and a pairwise ranking accuracy of 0.9787. These results confirm that the geometric and topological characteristics of shear wall layouts provide important information for evaluating cross-sectional design schemes.

Second, online local calibration effectively improved the layout-specific reliability of the global surrogate models. With 100 locally verified FEA samples, the Brier score of the feasibility model decreased from 0.0749 to 0.0437, and the MAE of the reinforcement usage model decreased from 11.48 t to 2.04 t. This indicates that the FEA results naturally generated during optimization can be used to correct local probability bias and reinforcement residuals without retraining the full graph neural network.

Third, the proposed calibrated surrogate-assisted strategy substantially reduced FEA calls while maintaining comparable solution quality. In optimization experiments on 15 test layouts with five random seeds, the strategy combining feasibility screening, cost-based ranking and online calibration reduced true FEA evaluations by approximately 64.6\% on average. Its feasible-solution discovery rate was 63.6\%, close to the 65.8\% achieved by the full FEA baseline. For cases where both methods found feasible solutions, the final material cost increased by only about 1.19\% on average. These results show that calibrated surrogate assistance can improve optimization efficiency while keeping the final design verification within the FEA-based code-checking workflow.

This study is limited to section and material optimization under given shear wall layouts. When the feasible region is extremely small or absent within the predefined design space, surrogate assistance can reduce ineffective evaluations but cannot resolve layout-level infeasibility. In addition, the effectiveness of local calibration depends on the availability and representativeness of FEA-verified samples accumulated during optimization. Future work will extend the framework to joint layout and section optimization, incorporate uncertainty-aware screening to better control false rejection risk, and further validate the method on more complex structural systems and design codes.
