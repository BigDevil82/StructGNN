\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study proposed CASIO, a calibrated surrogate-informed optimization framework, for cross-sectional and material optimization of shear wall structures. A layout-conditioned surrogate was developed to predict structural feasibility and reinforcement usage from room-level graph representations, while online local calibration was introduced to correct layout-specific prediction bias using FEA-verified samples accumulated during optimization.

The experimental results show that CASIO can improve structural optimization efficiency under fixed shear wall layouts, leading to the following conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Layout information improves surrogate accuracy. The proposed FuseGNN achieved a feasibility PR-AUC of 0.8593 and a reinforcement prediction $R^2$ of 0.924 on the test layouts, outperforming models using design parameters alone or with seven room-graph summaries. The comparison does not isolate graph encoding from the 41 additional layout statistics used by FuseGNN.

\item The separate local-calibration comparison indicates substantial potential for correcting layout-specific bias. With 100 randomly sampled local FEA records, the retrospectively selected calibrators reduced the feasibility Brier score from 0.0749 to 0.0437 and reinforcement MAE from 11.48~t to 2.04~t. These values characterize the model comparison, rather than the fixed online configuration.

\item The calibrated surrogate-informed strategy can reduce FEA demand with limited cost penalty. Across 15 test layouts and five random seeds, the Screen \& ranking strategy reduced true FEA evaluations by 64.6\% on average, with a 1.19\% mean cost increase across 142 paired feasible cases. The median increase was approximately zero and the maximum was 17.39\%; runs that failed to find a feasible design are excluded from these cost statistics.

\item The method is most effective when feasible candidates exist within the design space but evaluating the full candidate population by FEA is computationally prohibitive under the available budget.
\end{enumerate}

These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework while also highlighting several directions for further development. The main limitations are the fixed-layout setting, selection bias in the local feedback, and the absence of separate optimization ablations for local adaptation and matched FEA budgets. The reported model-selection comparisons also share layouts with the optimization benchmark and do not establish independent generalization of the chosen settings. Screening may miss feasible regions because rejected candidates are not evaluated. Future work will consider joint layout-section optimization, uncertainty-aware screening, and validation on more complex structural systems and design codes.
