\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

The experimental evaluation comprises three parts: (1) assessing the cross-layout accuracy and ranking quality of the global surrogates on unseen layouts; (2) examining local probability and residual calibration across varying local sample sizes; and (3) benchmarking the complete CASIO framework against full FEA optimization across multiple search algorithms.

\subsection{Dataset and Structural Evaluation}
\label{subsec:dataset_structural_evaluation}

The dataset consists of 143 real-world engineering shear wall layouts collected from architectural design practice~\cite{LengRoomGNN2026}. Following the dataset grouping and naming convention established in the previous study~\cite{Liao2021}, these layouts are categorized into three source groups: Group7-H1 (seismic intensity 7, peak ground acceleration (PGA) $0.10\text{ g}$, height $H \leq 50\text{ m}$), Group7-H2 (intensity 7, $H > 50\text{ m}$), and Group8 (intensity 8, PGA $0.20\text{ g}$).

For each layout, 5,000 design variants are sampled from the discrete parameter space in Table~\ref{tab:parameter_space}, enforcing nonincreasing wall thicknesses with height ($w_t \le w_m \le w_b$). Each candidate is evaluated by an automated structural evaluator $\mathcal{E}$ that builds the finite element model in OpenSees~\cite{McKenna2011OpenSees} (Fig.~\ref{fig:model_cases}), extracts global responses and member forces, and performs code-based reinforcement design and checking to obtain the binary feasibility label $y_f$ and reinforcement mass $m_s$. After discarding 28 non-convergent runs, 714,972 valid samples remain and are split at the layout level into 100 training, 22 validation, and 21 testing layouts (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_split}).

Material costs are calculated using fixed unit prices of CNY~5/kg for reinforcement and CNY~500, 530, 560, 590, and 620/m$^3$ for concrete grades C30--C50 (converted to mass prices at a density of $2400\text{ kg/m}^3$). Prior to FEA, concrete mass $\widetilde m_c(x)$ is computed analytically from member geometry, whereas final optimization costs use FEA-evaluated material quantities.

\subsection{Global Surrogate Model Training}
\label{subsec:global_surrogate_training}

The contribution of structural layout representations is evaluated by comparing three input configurations. \textbf{Param-MLP} uses only the 12 design and condition inputs, \textbf{Stat-MLP} adds the seven room-graph summaries without graph message passing, and \textbf{FuseGNN} combines the full room-adjacency graph, seven graph summaries, and 41 global layout statistics.

Both FuseGNN networks use three GraphSAGE layers trained independently with AdamW and batch size 512. The feasibility classifier $\Phi_f$ (hidden width 256, dropout 0.35, learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $3\times10^{-4}$) is trained with positive-weighted binary cross-entropy. Early stopping maximizes the validation rejection rate at a target feasible recall of $0.995$, yielding a global screening threshold of $0.004$. The reinforcement regressor $\Phi_s$ (hidden width 128, dropout 0.1, learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) minimizes Smooth L1 loss on log-transformed mass and uses validation mean absolute error (MAE) for early stopping.

Global models are evaluated on the 21 test layouts. Classification and screening are assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC), area under the precision--recall curve (PR-AUC), F1 score, rejection rate, feasibility recall, and false-reject count. Regression and ranking are assessed using MAE, root mean square error (RMSE), $R^2$, mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), bias, pairwise ranking accuracy, Spearman correlation, and top-10\% recall.

\subsection{Local Calibration Comparison and Online Configuration}
\label{subsec:local_calibration_settings}

To analyze sample efficiency prior to online deployment, an offline local-calibration study randomly samples $25, 50, 100, 200,$ or $500$ records from each test layout (repeated three times) to fit local calibrators, evaluating them on the remaining held-out records of that layout. Candidate feasibility calibrators include Platt scaling, isotonic regression~\cite{Zadrozny2002}, logistic regression, probability-residual models (ridge regression, random forest, LightGBM), and a probability-averaging ensemble~\cite{Bella2013}. Candidate reinforcement residual models include ridge regression~\cite{Hoerl1970Ridge}, Gaussian process regression~\cite{Rasmussen2006GPML}, LightGBM, and $k$-nearest neighbors~\cite{Cover1967KNN}. Probability reliability is measured by Brier score~\cite{Brier1950} and expected calibration error (ECE)~\cite{Naeini2015}, alongside screening and regression metrics.

For the deployed online optimization framework (Section~\ref{subsec:online_local_calibration}), feasibility calibration uses Platt scaling once at least 25 evaluated records spanning both classes are collected, with adaptive screening parameters $\kappa=0.995$ and $\alpha=0.1$. Reinforcement residual correction uses ridge regression for 25--49 valid residual records, Gaussian process regression for 50--199 records, and LightGBM for $\ge 200$ records. Full model hyperparameters are listed in \ref{app:implementation_details}.

\subsection{Optimization Benchmark Settings}
\label{subsec:optimization_benchmark_settings}

Optimization benchmarks are conducted on 15 unseen test layouts (five per design-condition group) across five random seeds, yielding 75 runs per strategy. Genetic algorithm (GA)~\cite{Goldberg1989} serves as the primary optimizer (population size 48, 15 generations, mutation rate $0.30$, elite size 2), supplemented by particle swarm optimization (PSO)~\cite{Kennedy1995PSO} (swarm size 48, 15 iterations, mutation rate $0.15$) and random search (720 candidates in batches of 48) to test optimizer generality.

Three candidate evaluation strategies are compared under identical design spaces and initial seeds:

(1) Full FEA: All generated candidates are evaluated by $\mathcal{E}$ (baseline).

(2) Cost ranking: Candidates are ranked solely by estimated material cost without feasibility screening, submitting only the top-quota subset to $\mathcal{E}$.

(3) Screen \& ranking: The full CASIO strategy combining calibrated feasibility screening, deficit-penalized cost ranking, and quota-based FEA allocation.

For surrogate-informed strategies, the batch quota $n_t$ uses a minimum quota $n_{\min}=8$ (capped by $|\mathcal{P}_t|$) and an evaluation ratio $\theta_t = 0.8$ before the first feasible solution is found, decreasing to $\theta_t = 0.5$ afterward. In the post-discovery stage, $n_t$ is split into 60\% priority-score ranking, 25\% smallest feasibility-score deficit, and 15\% random exploration within $\mathcal{P}_t$. Priority scoring uses $p_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.5$ and $\eta=10^6\text{ CNY}$. Unselected candidates receive $M_{\mathrm{rej}}=10^{12}$, and evaluated infeasible designs use penalty weights $P_{\mathrm{inf}}=10^5$, $\lambda_v=10^7$, and $\lambda_c=0.01$. Optimization performance is evaluated by uncached FEA call count, FEA reduction ratio, feasible-solution discovery rate, FEA calls to first feasible design, and paired final material cost ratio (\ref{app:evaluation_metrics}).
