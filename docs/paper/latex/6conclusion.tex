\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study develops a room-level conditional graph framework for shear wall layout generation in high-rise residential buildings. By treating rooms and room adjacencies as the basic units of prediction, the method aligns graph representation with architectural space semantics and with the boundary-constrained nature of shear wall placement. FiLM-based conditioning and dual-stream training further allow the model to respond to prescribed density regimes under sparse paired supervision.

Experiments on 143 high-rise residential floor plans show that this formulation improves Image IoU to 0.597 and exceeds the component-based GNN baseline by 9.8\%. Within this dataset, the main empirical finding is not only that the room-level representation improves overall accuracy, but also that it reduces spurious wall predictions and strengthens condition responsiveness across the three tested density groups. Finite-element case studies on two real projects provide additional, case-based evidence that the generated schemes can preserve acceptable global stiffness characteristics under the tested settings.

Future work should focus on three directions that follow directly from the current limits of the framework: supporting irregular polygonal rooms without decomposition, replacing discrete condition groups with richer continuous design descriptors, and coupling generation more tightly with structural-performance objectives during training.
