Revision:
- In Section 1: Therefore, this paper addressed two coupled challenges: introducing room-level semantics into graph-based generation and maintaining condition responsiveness under sparse paired supervision.
- In Section 3: To address the challenges of semantic alignment and conditional controllability in shear wall design, this paper presented a unified generative framework, RC-GNN (Room-level Conditional Graph Neural Network), as illustrated in Fig. 1.
- In Section 3.1.1: An adjacency edge e_ij is added to E only if l_ij exceeds a physical threshold tau_adj, which was set to 400 mm in this paper.
- In Section 3.1.3: However, the open-source dataset employed in this paper did not include room-type annotations for individual spaces.

- In Section 3.4.2: In this paper, density was not defined as a normalized area ratio in [0,1].
- In Section 4.1.1: The dataset used in this paper was restricted to plans for which the architectural drawing, the engineered shear wall drawing, and the condition label can be aligned reliably after preprocessing.
- In Section 4.1.1: It should be noted that the condition label used in this paper is a discrete density category rather than a continuous engineering descriptor.
- In Section 4.1.2: The experimental comparison focuses on graph-based methods because the central question addressed in this paper was whether a room-level graph representation can improve graph-based shear wall layout prediction compared with the commonly used component-level graph representation.
- In Section 5.2: Regarding the scope of application, this paper focused on high-rise residential buildings in seismic regions, a typology where shear wall layouts are strictly governed by room partitions.
- In Section 6: This paper presented a room-level conditional graph framework for shear wall layout generation in high-rise residential buildings.
